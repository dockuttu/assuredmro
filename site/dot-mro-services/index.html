What a DOT MRO does

Under the Department of Transportation’s testing rules in 49 CFR Part 40, the Medical Review Officer is the independent physician who stands between the laboratory and the employer. The lab reports a result; the MRO decides whether that result is verified before the designated employer representative (DER) ever sees it.

  • Review of every result and custody form. We check each laboratory result against the Federal Custody and Control Form and correct or cancel tests when the paperwork has a fatal or uncorrected flaw.
  • Donor interviews for non-negative results. The MRO personally contacts the donor to give them the chance to present a legitimate medical explanation, such as a valid prescription, before a positive result is verified.
  • Verified reporting to the DER. Negatives are released quickly. Verified positives, refusals, adulterated and substituted results are reported confidentially, with the drug or reason stated.
  • Split specimen coordination. A donor has 72 hours after the MRO notifies them of a verified result to request a test of the split specimen at a second HHS-certified laboratory. We manage the request and report the outcome.
  • Shy bladder and insufficient breath reviews. When a donor can’t provide enough urine or breath, we review the referral evaluation and determine whether there is an adequate medical explanation.
  • Safety concern reporting. If a donor’s legally prescribed medication could make them medically unqualified or pose a significant safety risk, Part 40 allows the MRO to report that concern, and we handle it carefully and by the rule.

FMCSA Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse reporting

For CDL drivers, MROs must report verified positive, adulterated and substituted results, and certain refusals, to the FMCSA Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse within the timeframe the rule sets. We handle these entries as part of your per-result price, so employers and C/TPAs don’t have to chase the MRO for Clearinghouse entries.

Qualifications you can verify

49 CFR 40.121 requires an MRO to be a licensed physician who has completed MRO qualification training, passed an examination given by an HHS-approved MRO certification board, requalifies every five years and keeps up continuing education. Assured MRO’s medical review officer, Robin Arora, MD, is certified by the American Association of Medical Review Officers (AAMRO) through August 2027. Because DOT rules let a qualified MRO review results for covered employees wherever they are located, we serve DOT programs nationwide.

FAQ

Common questions

Can one MRO review DOT tests in every state?

Yes. Under 49 CFR 40.121, a licensed physician who meets the MRO training, examination and requalification requirements can act as MRO for DOT-covered employees wherever they are located.

What happens when a DOT test comes back non-negative?

The MRO reviews the lab report and custody form, then contacts the donor for a confidential interview. If there is a legitimate medical explanation, such as a valid prescription, the result is reported as negative. If not, it is verified and reported to the employer.

Do you report to the FMCSA Clearinghouse?

Yes. For CDL drivers we report verified positive, adulterated and substituted results, and applicable refusals, to the Clearinghouse as the rule requires. It is included in the per-result price.

How long does a donor have to request a split specimen test?

Under Part 40, the donor has 72 hours after the MRO notifies them of a verified positive, adulterated or substituted result to request a test of the split specimen.

Do we have to change our lab or collection sites?

Usually not. We work with HHS-certified laboratories and your existing collection network. You designate Assured MRO as your MRO and we handle the rest.
