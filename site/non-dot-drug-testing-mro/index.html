Why non-DOT programs use an MRO

Employers aren’t always required to use a Medical Review Officer for non-DOT testing, but most well-run programs do. A lab result alone can’t tell you whether a donor is taking a legitimately prescribed medication. An MRO review turns a raw lab result into a verified result that holds up when a donor challenges it, and it keeps donors’ medical information away from supervisors.

  • Donor interviews. The MRO contacts every donor with a non-negative result and reviews prescriptions and medical explanations confidentially.
  • Verified results only. The employer receives a verified negative, a verified positive, or a test cancellation, without the donor’s medical details.
  • Consistent documentation. Every review is documented the same way, which matters if a decision is later questioned.

Panels we review

Non-DOT programs choose their own panels. We review standard 5-panel and 10-panel tests and expanded panels your lab offers, including expanded opioids, fentanyl, benzodiazepines, barbiturates and other drugs your program adds. We review urine, oral fluid and hair results when your laboratory reports them to an MRO.

State law matters for non-DOT testing

For non-DOT testing, state law sets many of the rules: which employers can test, what notice is required, whether a confirmation test is required, and how marijuana results may be used. Rules on marijuana in particular vary widely from state to state, and some states protect off-duty use for certain employees. We follow your written policy and flag anything unusual during setup, but your program’s legal obligations are for you and your counsel to decide.

FAQ

Common questions

Is an MRO required for non-DOT drug testing?

Not always. Requirements depend on your state and your policy. Many employers use an MRO anyway because a verified result is more defensible and keeps donors’ medical information confidential.

Do you review fentanyl and expanded opioid panels?

Yes. We review whatever panel your laboratory runs and reports to an MRO, including fentanyl and expanded opioids.

How do you handle marijuana results for non-DOT programs?

We verify the result and report it according to your written policy. How an employer may act on a marijuana result depends on state law, which varies, so check your policy with counsel.

Can you review oral fluid and hair tests?

Yes, when your laboratory performs the test and reports the result for MRO review.

Is non-DOT pricing different from DOT pricing?

No. The flat per-result price is the same for DOT and non-DOT results, negative or positive.
